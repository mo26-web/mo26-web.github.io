Why I Started Writing About My Research

Papers are the official record of research, but they are also a heavily edited one. By the time a method reaches a journal, the false starts have been pruned, the engineering compromises tidied away, and the messy intuition that actually drove the work has been rewritten into clean, past-tense certainty. This blog is where I want to keep some of what gets cut.

My work sits at the intersection of machine learning and process control — fault detection, causal discovery, and decision support for industrial systems. A lot of the interesting decisions in that space never show up in the final write-up: why one causal framework was abandoned for another, how a model that looked great offline fell apart on real plant data, what a constraint actually means once you try to encode it.

What I plan to write about

A few recurring themes I expect to come back to:

  • Method notes — short explanations of techniques I use, written the way I wish they'd been explained to me.
  • Engineering trade-offs — the unglamorous decisions behind getting research code to run on real data.
  • Reading notes — papers I found worth the time, and why.
